2. Nearest neighbors#

The nearest neighbors rankers predict for each instance \(\boldsymbol{x}\) a ranking that aggregates the rankings \(\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k\) of its \(k\) nearest training samples \(\boldsymbol{x}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{x}_k\), numbered from the nearest one, with the notation of Notation. There is one for each problem:

  • KNeighborsLabelRanker, for label ranking, estimates a probability model of the rankings from those of the neighbors, which have no ties, and predicts its most probable ranking, as described in Label ranking.

  • KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker, for partial label ranking, aggregates the rankings of the neighbors, which may have ties, into a consensus bucket order, as described in Partial label ranking.

Both find and weigh the neighbors as the next section describes. The rankings follow the representation described in Ranking representation; the training rankings of both estimators may be incomplete, and their predictions are always complete.

2.1. Neighbor search and weights#

The neighbors are found by NearestNeighbors, which receives the parameters n_neighbors, algorithm, leaf_size, metric, p, metric_params and n_jobs, so the search algorithms, the metrics, metric="precomputed" and the kneighbors and kneighbors_graph methods are those described in the nearest neighbors section of the scikit-learn user guide. The n_jobs threads also aggregate the rankings of the neighbors of the samples in parallel.

The weights parameter weighs the rankings of the neighbors of each sample in their aggregation. "uniform", "distance" and a callable weigh them as in nearest neighbors classification. "dudani" follows equation (1) of [dudani_distance_1976], which [cheng_decision_2009] use: if \(d_{(1)} \leq \dots \leq d_{(k)}\) are the sorted distances \(d(\boldsymbol{x}, \boldsymbol{x}_i)\) of the neighbors \(\boldsymbol{x}_i\) to the instance \(\boldsymbol{x}\), the \(i\)-th nearest neighbor weighs

\[w_i = \frac{d_{(k)} - d_{(i)}}{d_{(k)} - d_{(1)}},\]

from 1 for the nearest neighbor to 0 for the farthest one, or 1 if \(d_{(k)} = d_{(1)}\).

A callable may give a zero weight to all the neighbors of a sample. Then, the neighbors are weighted equally, except with the Mallows model of KNeighborsLabelRanker, which first tries the weights described in Weights.

2.2. Label ranking#

KNeighborsLabelRanker implements the instance-based label rankers of [cheng_decision_2009] and [cheng_label_2010]. They assume that the probability distribution of the rankings is approximately constant around each instance \(\boldsymbol{x}\), so the rankings of its \(k\) nearest neighbors are taken as a sample of a probability model of the rankings. The parameters of the model are estimated from these rankings, and the predicted ranking is the mode of the model, its most probable ranking:

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from sklr.neighbors import KNeighborsLabelRanker
>>> X = [[0], [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]
>>> y = [[1, 2, 3], [2, 1, 3], [1, 3, 2], [np.nan, 1, 2], [2, np.nan, 1], [3, 2, 1]]
>>> ranker = KNeighborsLabelRanker(n_neighbors=3).fit(X, y)
>>> ranker.predict([[0.4], [2.6], [4.2]])
array([[1, 2, 3],
       [1, 3, 2],
       [3, 2, 1]])

The training rankings may be incomplete, even without any ranked label, but they must not have ties, and the predicted rankings have no ties either. The model parameter chooses the probability model of the rankings of the neighbors, "mallows" for the Mallows model and "plackett_luce" for the Plackett-Luce model. In both cases, ties between labels in the predicted ranking go to the label with the smaller index. The neighbors are found and weighted as described in Neighbor search and weights.

2.2.1. Mallows model#

The Mallows model is a distance-based model whose mode is a center ranking \(\pi\): the probability of a ranking decreases with its distance to \(\pi\), at a rate given by a spread parameter \(\theta \geq 0\) [cheng_label_2010]. With model="mallows", the predicted ranking is the estimate \(\hat{\pi}\) of \(\pi\), found as in Algorithm 1 of [cheng_decision_2009].

When the rankings of the neighbors are complete, \(\hat{\pi}\) is found by the weighted Borda count [ho_decision_1994]. Each ranking \(\sigma_i\) gives \(n - \sigma_i(j) + 1\) votes to each label \(y_j\), that is, \(n\) votes to its first label, \(n - 1\) to the second one and so on, each vote is multiplied by the weight \(w_i\) of the neighbor, and the labels are ranked by decreasing total votes. When some rankings are incomplete, the initial center is found by the generalized Borda count of Proposition 2 of [cheng_decision_2009]: if \(\sigma_i\) ranks \(m = |\mathcal{Y}(\sigma_i)| \leq n\) labels, each ranked label \(y_j\) receives \((m - \sigma_i(j) + 1)(n + 1) / (m + 1)\) votes and each unranked label receives \((n + 1) / 2\), its expected votes when the ranking is completed uniformly at random. Then, each incomplete ranking is completed with its closest extension to the center in Kendall distance, found as in Proposition 1 of [cheng_decision_2009], and the center is found again by the Borda count of the completed rankings. These two steps are repeated until the center no longer changes.

[cheng_decision_2009] also estimate \(\theta\) as a measure of the reliability of the prediction, which KNeighborsLabelRanker does not compute.

2.2.2. Plackett-Luce model#

The Plackett-Luce model has a positive parameter \(v_j\) for each label \(y_j\), which form the vector \(\boldsymbol{v} = (v_1, \dots, v_n)\). If a ranking \(\pi\) ranks \(m = |\mathcal{Y}(\pi)|\) labels and \(\pi^{-1}(i)\) denotes the index of the label at position \(i\), its probability is

\[P(\pi \mid \boldsymbol{v}) = \prod_{i=1}^{m} \frac{v_{\pi^{-1}(i)}}{\sum_{l=i}^{m} v_{\pi^{-1}(l)}},\]

which has the same form for complete and incomplete rankings [cheng_label_2010]. With model="plackett_luce", the parameters are the maximum likelihood estimate from the rankings of the neighbors, which maximizes \(\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_i \log P(\sigma_i \mid \boldsymbol{v})\) for the rankings \(\sigma_i\) and the weights \(w_i\) of the neighbors, the log-likelihood of [cheng_label_2010] when the weights are uniform. It is found by the MM algorithm of [hunter_mm_2004], which starts from equal parameters, scales them to add up to one after each iteration and stops when no parameter changes by more than tol. The predicted ranking, the mode of the model, sorts the labels by decreasing parameter [cheng_label_2010]:

>>> ranker = KNeighborsLabelRanker(n_neighbors=3, model="plackett_luce").fit(X, y)
>>> ranker.predict([[0.4], [2.6], [4.2]])
array([[1, 2, 3],
       [1, 3, 2],
       [3, 1, 2]])

A label that no neighbor with a positive weight ranks above another label gets a zero parameter, so it goes after the other labels, and all the labels get the same parameter, which predicts them in the order of their indices, if no such neighbor ranks two labels.

2.2.3. Weights#

Weighting the neighbors changes the prediction of the second sample of the first example:

>>> ranker = KNeighborsLabelRanker(n_neighbors=3, weights="distance").fit(X, y)
>>> ranker.predict([[0.4], [2.6], [4.2]])
array([[1, 2, 3],
       [1, 2, 3],
       [3, 2, 1]])

With model="mallows", the weight of each neighbor is also multiplied by the fraction \(|\mathcal{Y}(\sigma_i)| / n\) of the \(n\) labels that its ranking \(\sigma_i\) ranks, where \(\mathcal{Y}(\sigma_i)\) is the set of those labels, as in [cheng_decision_2009]: a ranking completed by the estimation is less reliable than an observed one. If these products are zero for all the neighbors of a sample, as with weights="dudani" when only the farthest neighbor ranks some label, the neighbors are weighted by that fraction alone, and equally if it is also zero, because no neighbor ranks any label. The Plackett-Luce model does not complete the rankings, so its weights are not multiplied by that fraction.

2.2.4. Convergence#

The estimation of both models is iterative, and max_iter bounds the number of iterations for each sample. By default, it is 100 with model="mallows" and 1000 with model="plackett_luce". If the estimation of some sample does not converge, predict() issues a single ConvergenceWarning and returns the rankings reached.

With model="mallows", the estimation only iterates when some neighbor has an incomplete ranking, and it does not converge when the center still changes after max_iter iterations or comes back to an earlier center, which the algorithm would repeat forever.

With model="plackett_luce", the maximum likelihood estimate exists, and the MM algorithm converges to it, if, for every split of the labels into two groups, some label of the second group is ranked above some label of the first one [hunter_mm_2004]. Otherwise, as when the rankings of the neighbors agree or a label is never ranked above another one, the parameters slowly approach a limit where some of them are zero, and the warning is common when the neighbors are few. The predicted ranking then sorts the labels by the parameters reached after max_iter iterations. A larger tol lets the estimation stop earlier, and a larger max_iter lets it get closer to that limit.

2.2.5. Complexity#

After the neighbor search, for each sample with \(k\) neighbors and \(n\) labels, the Borda count takes \(O(kn)\) time plus a sort of the labels, each iteration of the Mallows model with incomplete rankings takes at most \(O(kn^2)\) time to complete them, and each iteration of the MM algorithm takes \(O(kn)\) time.

2.3. Partial label ranking#

KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker implements the instance-based partial label ranker of [alfaro_learning_2021]. The training rankings may have ties, and the prediction for an instance \(\boldsymbol{x}\) is a bucket order, an ordered partition of the labels into buckets of tied labels, that aggregates the rankings of its \(k\) nearest neighbors:

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from sklr.neighbors import KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker
>>> X = [[0], [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]
>>> y = [
...     [1, 2, 2, np.nan],
...     [1, 1, 2, np.nan],
...     [1, 2, 3, np.nan],
...     [np.nan, 1, 1, 2],
...     [2, np.nan, 1, 1],
...     [3, 2, 1, np.nan],
... ]
>>> ranker = KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker(n_neighbors=3).fit(X, y)
>>> ranker.predict([[0.4], [4.2]])
array([[1, 2, 3, 4],
       [3, 1, 1, 2]])

No neighbor of the first sample ranks the fourth label, so it goes alone to a last bucket, as explained in Labels without information. The neighbors are found and weighted as described in Neighbor search and weights.

2.3.1. Consensus bucket order#

Here, the neighbors are indexed by \(l\), since \(i\) and \(j\) index the labels. For each pair of labels \(y_i\) and \(y_j\), the entry \(C(y_i, y_j)\) of the pair order matrix of the neighbors is the weighted fraction of their rankings ranking both labels that put \(y_i\) ahead of \(y_j\), with a ranking that ties them counting one half, as in equation (3) of [alfaro_learning_2021] with the weights \(w_l\) of the neighbors:

\[C(y_i, y_j) = \frac{\sum_{l} w_l \, [\sigma_l(i) < \sigma_l(j)] + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{l} w_l \, [\sigma_l(i) = \sigma_l(j)]}{\sum_{l} w_l},\]

where the sums run over the neighbors whose rankings rank both labels, a condition in brackets is 1 when it holds and 0 otherwise, and \(C(y_i, y_j) = 1/2\) if no neighbor of positive weight ranks both labels. Thus, \(C(y_i, y_j) + C(y_j, y_i) = 1\). The weights are not multiplied by the fraction of the labels that each ranking ranks, since the consensus does not complete the rankings. A bucket order \(\pi\) has the bucket matrix \(B\) with \(B(y_i, y_j)\) equal to 1, 1/2 or 0 when \(\pi\) puts \(y_i\) ahead of \(y_j\), ties them or puts \(y_j\) ahead, and the optimal bucket order problem looks for the bucket order whose bucket matrix minimizes \(\sum_{i, j} |B(y_i, y_j) - C(y_i, y_j)|\), as in equation (1) of [aledo_utopia_2017].

The predicted bucket order \(\hat\pi\) is an approximate solution found by the Bucket Pivot Algorithm. It chooses a pivot label \(y_p\) and puts each other label \(y_j\) in a group of labels ahead of the pivot if \(C(y_p, y_j) < 1/2 - \beta\), in the bucket of the pivot if \(1/2 - \beta \leq C(y_p, y_j) \leq 1/2 + \beta\), and in a group of labels behind the pivot otherwise, with the threshold \(\beta = 0.25\). Then, it applies itself to the labels ahead and to the labels behind, and the bucket order is the one of the labels ahead, followed by the bucket of the pivot and by the one of the labels behind. KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker uses the variant \(\mathrm{LIA}_G^{\mathrm{MP2}}\) of [aledo_utopia_2017], which [alfaro_learning_2021] chose for its trade-off between accuracy and efficiency:

  • With the least indecision assumption, the pivot is the label with the least DANI (Distance to Achieve the Nearest Idealization) index. The utopian matrix rounds each entry \(C(y_i, y_j)\) to 1 if it is above 0.75, to 0 if it is below 0.25 and to 1/2 otherwise, and the DANI index of \(y_i\) is the mean absolute difference between \(C(y_i, y_j)\) and its rounded value over the other labels \(y_j\), which is small when the neighbors agree on how \(y_i\) compares with the rest. The rounding follows Example 1 of [aledo_utopia_2017], since its Definition 1 rounds \(C(y_j, y_i)\) instead.

  • The DANI indices are computed once, among all the labels being ordered, and not again for the labels ahead and behind (the subscript \(G\), for global).

  • With multiple pivots, each label placed in the bucket of the pivot joins it as a pivot, and the next labels are compared with the mean of \(C(y_l, y_j)\) over the pivots \(y_l\) instead of with \(C(y_p, y_j)\). In a second stage, the labels ahead and behind are placed again with the mean over the final pivots.

[aledo_utopia_2017] choose at random among the labels with the least DANI index. KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker chooses the one with the smaller index, and places the other labels in the order of their indices, which matters because the pivots grow, so its predictions are deterministic.

2.3.2. Labels without information#

A label that no neighbor of positive weight ranks together with another label has all its entries of \(C\) equal to 1/2, hence the least DANI index, and as the first pivot it would draw all the labels into its bucket. Neither [alfaro_learning_2021] nor [aledo_utopia_2017] consider this case. Therefore, only the other labels are ordered by the algorithm, with their DANI indices computed among them, and the labels without information are tied in a last bucket. If no neighbor of positive weight ranks two labels, all the labels are tied.

2.3.3. Complexity#

After the neighbor search, for each sample with \(k\) neighbors and \(n\) labels, the pair order matrix takes \(O(kn^2)\) time, and the DANI indices and the Bucket Pivot Algorithm take \(O(n^2)\) time, since each label becomes a pivot once and then updates the sums of at most \(n\) labels.

2.4. Practical use#

Both estimators are evaluated and tuned with the tools of scikit-learn, as shown in Getting started, with the scorers described in Metrics and scoring: evaluating the rankers: "kendall_tau" for KNeighborsLabelRanker and "tau_x" for KNeighborsPartialLabelRanker. [cheng_decision_2009], [cheng_label_2010] and [alfaro_learning_2021] use the Euclidean distance and choose the number of neighbors by cross-validation on the training data. The first two normalize the attributes, as feature scaling does, and the last one weighs the neighbors by the inverse of their distance, which is weights="distance".

The examples Label ranking with the k-nearest neighbors and Partial label ranking with the k-nearest neighbors compare the models and the weights of each estimator for several numbers of neighbors, also with incomplete training rankings, and Reproduction of the papers compares the results of KNeighborsLabelRanker with those published in [cheng_decision_2009] and [cheng_label_2010].
